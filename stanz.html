Kenya Wildlife Service, which is a state corporation established in 1989 to conserve and manage Kenya's wildlife, national parks, reserves, and sanctuaries.

History of KWS

• Pre-Colonial Era: Indigenous sustainable land management supported rich and abundant wildlife across the region. • Colonial Period: Formal hunting laws, settlement expansions, and sport hunting introduced by colonial authorities caused major wildlife population declines. • 1945–1946: The Royal National Parks Ordinance led to the creation of Nairobi National Park in 1946, marking the first national park in Kenya and East Africa. • 1963–1977: Following independence, the nation recognized wildlife as a core national heritage, culminating in a total national hunting ban in 1977. • 1970s–1980s (The Rhino Crisis): Rampant poaching severely depleted wildlife, notably reducing Tsavo's black rhino population from over 8,000 to fewer than 20. • 1989 (Inception & Ivory Burning): President Daniel arap Moi appointed Richard Leakey to head the precursor Wildlife Conservation and Management Department. Leakey formed armed anti-poaching units and oversaw the historic burning of 12 tons of confiscated ivory in Nairobi National Park to draw global attention to the crisis.
